# Fraud Labs · Sources checked

Checked on 24 November 2026.

1. **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** — https://cybercrime.gov.in/  
   Government portal used in the help flow.
2. **Government instructions for reporting financial cyber fraud** — https://cybercrime.gov.in/UploadMedia/instructions_citizenreportingcyberfrauds.pdf  
   Confirms that victims can call 1930 or report through cybercrime.gov.in. The instructions also describe keeping transaction details and screenshots. The PDF is labelled for Delhi; the national portal itself is linked separately.
3. **Reserve Bank of India, BE(A)WARE booklet** — https://m.rbi.org.in/commonperson/Upload/english/Content/PDFs/English%20BEAWARE.pdf  
   RBI booklet describing common financial-fraud methods and precautions.
4. **Reserve Bank of India, Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct Directions** — https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13140  
   The current directions reviewed contain customer-protection and reporting provisions. Exact liability depends on the facts and the applicable bank rules.
5. **Reserve Bank of India, Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct Third Amendment Directions, 2026** — https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13543  
   Issued 24 June 2026; the amendment states that it applies to transactions undertaken on or after 1 January 2027. This makes a blanket “zero liability if reported within three working days” statement unsuitable for this general-audience module.
6. **NPCI, UPI FAQs** — https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi/faqs  
   Official UPI FAQ source. The module's simple payment-PIN safety wording is also consistent with the broader RBI BE(A)WARE advice not to share confidential payment credentials.

## Facts included

- 1930 is presented as the cyber financial-fraud helpline.
- cybercrime.gov.in is presented as the government reporting portal.
- Users are advised to contact their bank immediately, ask it to block exposed payment access, and preserve screenshots and transaction details.
- Users are warned never to share OTP, PIN or CVV, and never to enter a UPI PIN to receive money.
- Each practice example now explains the scammer’s likely goal and one safer next step. These are educational interpretations of the fictional scenario, not claims about a real case.
- The map adds three practical checks: pause, verify and protect.
- The help screen lists evidence details that can help a person explain the incident when reporting.

## Facts deliberately left out

- No promise of reimbursement, recovery or zero customer liability.
- No “three working days means zero liability” shortcut. RBI's framework is conditional, and the 2026 amendment has a future effective date.
- No consumer helpline 1915, because the four-screen flow is specifically for cyber financial fraud and the source review did not establish a necessary role for that number here.
