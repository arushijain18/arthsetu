# 30-minute community facilitator guide

## Before the session

Bring blank paper, a pen and paper tokens labelled ₹50, ₹100 and ₹200. Show the module on a phone if available. Do not collect completed worksheets. Do not photograph participants' plans. Do not ask for Aadhaar, PAN, bank account numbers, PINs, OTPs, income, loan balances or family hardship. Participants may pass on any question.

## Session plan

### 0–4 minutes · Welcome without judgement

Say: “Many people earn daily, weekly or after a job is finished. Some days are good and some are slow. Today we will learn an order for money. We will not compare how much anyone earns.”

Ask the group to name types of work in the area: street selling, domestic work, transport, repair, shops, delivery, tailoring, design or other work. Do not ask who does which job.

### 4–9 minutes · Tell a short story

“Ramesh sells vegetables. On a good day he brings home ₹600. On a weak day he brings home ₹200. Before spending at home, he must keep enough for tomorrow's vegetables and transport. If he spends that money tonight, he may not be able to earn tomorrow.”

Ask: “What must Ramesh protect first?” Introduce the order: work money → essential home costs → buffer for a weak period → extra lifestyle spending.

### 9–15 minutes · Find the Floor Number

Draw four lines on a blank sheet:

1. Housing / rent
2. Ration + utilities
3. School + essential health
4. Work cost: stock, fuel, tools or travel

Say: “This is the minimum one-month amount needed for the home and the work to continue. It is not a wish list. Use pretend numbers in this group. Keep your own numbers private.”

Let each person quietly add the four amounts on their own page. Do not ask anyone to read the total aloud.

### 15–22 minutes · Practise with paper money

Use the tier that best suits the example.

- Daily earner: Work First → Home Fixed → Today.
- Weekly or shop earner: Business → Personal draw → Buffer.
- Invoice worker: Tax reserve → Business costs → Personal draw → Buffer.

Give a sample amount, such as ₹600, and ask the group to place tokens into the boxes. The amounts are only for practice. Ask: “What amount must be kept to earn again?” “Which home costs cannot be delayed?” “What can be held for a weak day?”

For the shop example, compare total sales with fuel, stock, maintenance, platform fees or wastage. Explain that sales are not the same as personal income.

### 22–27 minutes · Repeat the five questions

Read each line slowly. Ask participants to point to the matching number or repeat the order:

1. Can I keep earning tomorrow?
2. Are essential bills covered?
3. Is my buffer growing?
4. Do I owe tax or business costs?
5. Only now: can I spend or invest more?

Emphasise that tax and business costs may apply differently to different people. No one should copy another worker's split.

### 27–30 minutes · One private next step

Ask everyone to choose one small habit for the next seven days. Examples: write the minimum household cost, keep tomorrow's work money separate, save some of a strong day for a weak day, or write the invoice due date.

Share the official support links in the safety-net page. Explain that eligibility is checked through official portals or local government offices. Thank the group.

## Safeguarding and facilitation notes

- Never promise a loan, pension, benefit, job card or insurance claim.
- Do not shame anyone for a low-income month or debt.
- Do not tell anyone to stop paying a debt or ignore a legal notice.
- Do not suggest that a government registration automatically pays cash.
- Do not pressure a participant to invest. If the Floor Number is not covered, prioritise earning ability and essentials.
- If a participant asks for personalised tax, debt, insurance or legal advice, refer them to a qualified professional or official help channel.
- A participant may keep the plan private and may leave the session at any time.
